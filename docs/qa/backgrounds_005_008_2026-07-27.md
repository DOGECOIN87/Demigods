# QA — Backgrounds 005 to 008 registered (2026-07-27)

DG-011 to DG-014. `backgrounds` goes from 4 to 8, which is the single most
valuable thing that could be added to the collection right now — see below.

## Why these mattered more than their count

`scripts/build_token_sheet.py --salience` measures how much swapping one category
changes a 210 px thumbnail. With 4 backgrounds registered:

| Category | Assets | Mean thumbnail delta |
|---|---|---|
| backgrounds | 4 | **47.1** |
| outfits | 6 | 11.8 |
| hair_front | 8 | 4.9 |
| rear_auras | 6 | 2.0 |
| base_bodies | 5 | 0.5 |
| expression_marks | 8 | 0.3 |
| eyes | 13 | 0.2 |
| eyebrows | 9 | 0.1 |
| mouths | 13 | 0.1 |

Only three categories move a thumbnail at all, and backgrounds move it four times
harder than anything else. At 4 × 6 × 8 that was **192 distinct appearances**;
777 tokens drawn from 192 gives about 189 distinct looks, so roughly 588 tokens
would have shared their appearance with another. Doubling backgrounds doubles
that ceiling to 384.

The 560-million combination space says nothing about this. Most of it is eyes,
mouths and expression marks, which are invisible at marketplace size.

## The source problem, and what was measured before waiving the rule

Backgrounds 001 to 004 were registered from **native 1254 renders** kept in
`images/background_candidates/native_candidates/`. No such render exists for 005
to 008 — only the 1024 × 1024 JPEG references, which the validator correctly
flags as reference-only and which `prompts/17` says must never be upscaled to
reach the canvas.

The collection owner waived the upscale rule. Rather than take that on trust, the
cost was measured, and this category turns out to be the one place where it is
close to free — because `apply_background_depth.py` applies a **3.0 px Gaussian
blur** to every background, which is a far larger footprint than a 1.22× resample.

Two experiments, both run on the native 1254 candidates where the ground truth is
available:

| Route | Mean diff | p99 | Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| native 1254 → depth pass | — | — | — |
| native → 1024 → 1254 → depth pass | **0.24** | 1 | 5 |
| native → 1024 JPEG q90 → 1254 → depth pass | **0.60** | 2 | 6 |

Out of 255. The blur destroys more detail than the round-trip loses, so a
background routed through 1024 is indistinguishable from one that never left
1254.

A third measurement is worth recording because it is the one that could mislead:
comparing the *registered* 001–004 against a treated upscale of their own
references gives mean differences of 19.8 to 41.1. That is not resampling loss —
the native candidates are separately generated images that follow the reference
design rather than copying it. Reading that number as "upscaling costs 40" would
have been wrong, and it was the first thing measured.

Each entry records `origin: reference_upscale` and
`lanczos_upscale_1024_to_1254` in `postprocessing`, so these four never get
mistaken for native renders.

## Effect on the eye-palette contrast gate

`build_face_recolours.py --check-contrast` still passes, but the margins
tightened. The two new hue bands are background 007 at 12.5° and background 008
at 258.9°, and the closest palettes are now:

| Palette | Hue gap | Nearest background |
|---|---|---|
| rose | 40.5 | 007 golden celestial gateway |
| orchid | 41.1 | 008 violet void portal |

Against a floor of 40. Adding another warm-gold or violet background will break
one of them, and the gate will name it.

## Known limits

Background 005 is a bright daylight scene and sits noticeably lighter than the
other seven after grading. It reads as intended, but a pale outfit against it has
less separation than the same outfit against 002 or 008.

Six backgrounds remain: DG-158 to DG-163, which have no references yet.
